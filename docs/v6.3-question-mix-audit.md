## 1. Evidence and definitions

Three different questions must remain separate:

1. **What the current generator does:** enumerate the actual canonical family pools in `scripts/math-activities.js`, using the shared classifier. Within each complete balanced round it emits every family once, then starts another round. Reverse addition/multiplication variants share family weight. Recent-family avoidance affects sequence, not an intentional level quota.
2. **What recorded practice showed:** the owner's private testing motivated the review and indicated excessive earlier-level content in several Level 3 activities. The supplied handoff contains observations from one learner, not a controlled study or age benchmark. Its measured practice duration is not interchangeable with total hands-on testing time. Real learner statistics, exports, screenshots and private diagnostics are deliberately not reproduced in repository artifacts; compare the supplied handoff privately. No fresh backup was read for this audit.
3. **What the proposed generator should do:** apply explicit, activity-specific allocation where cumulative pools dilute selected-tier exposure, and retain already focused pools. Proposed percentages are product trial defaults, not validated educational optima or proof of improved learning.

Level numbers below are user-facing 1–5. “Earliest eligible level” is mathematical pool membership, not cognitive difficulty. For example, multiplication reversal can make a family eligible earlier than its displayed first operand suggests. Division remains ordered; bond totals remain distinct.

The tier columns in section 3 include identities and sum to 100% (apart from display rounding). The identity column is a **subset**, not an additional share. Section 4 instead removes identities first: its ordinary selected-tier, ordinary earlier-review and identity buckets are mutually exclusive. Use that second definition for allocation targets.

“Current mix” means the exact share of canonical families in the allowed pool, and hence a complete balanced round. It does not promise those percentages in each five-question flight or in a finite learner history. Pool size also determines how soon repeated families become unavoidable. Restarting a session creates a fresh round; the existing immediate Practice Again preference favors unused families from the preceding flight, not an unlimited cross-session allocation ledger.

**Fact-type glossary — all currently practiced activities**

“Ordinary” is the audit shorthand for **non-identity**: a valid question without one of the narrow zero/identity flags below. It does not mean difficult, unfamiliar or useful to every learner. “Freebie” is an informal owner-facing term, not a reliable mathematical or learning category; use the named flag instead in production explanations.

| Activity | Ordinary examples | Zero/identity patterns included in this audit | Important distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Addition | 2+3, 8+7, 24+36 | A zero addend: 0+n or n+0, including 0+0 | Adding zero preserves the other number. 1+n and doubles such as 4+4 are ordinary here. |
| Subtraction | 8−3, 15−7, 64−28 | Subtract zero (n−0); equal operands (n−n), including 0−0 | Subtracting zero preserves the number; subtracting itself gives zero. These flags overlap at 0−0 but the question counts once. |
| Multiplication | 2×5, 7×8, 16×7 | Unit factor: 1×n or n×1. Zero-factor flag also defined for 0×n/n×0, but zero factors are not in current practice pools | ×2, ×5 and ×10 remain ordinary. Choosing table 1 intentionally asks for identities; no ordinary-practice identity cap should defeat that choice. |
| Division | 12÷3, 56÷7, 96÷6 | Equal operands n÷n=1. Unit divisor n÷1 and zero dividend 0÷n are defined flags but absent from current numbered pools | Current division is exact whole-number division, no remainders. Division by zero is invalid, never a freebie. Equal operands yield one, not zero. |
| Number bonds | 3+?=10, ?+7=20 | Known zero: 0+?=total; missing zero: total+?=total; either unknown position | The total is part of the scope. Finding a missing part is different from a standard addition prompt. |
| Doubles | Double 1, Double 7, Double 24 | Double 0=0 | Double 1 is ordinary under this narrow rule. Conceptual equivalence with multiplication does not merge activity records. |
| Halves | Half of 2, Half of 14, Half of 48 | Half of 0=0 | Current inputs are even whole numbers. Half of 2 is ordinary; no fractional-answer practice is included. |
| Squares | 2², 11², 16² | 1²=1; 0²=0 is a defined flag but absent from current pools | Squares start at base 1. The ordinary flag does not mean 2² is as demanding as 16². |

Other useful classifications are **independent axes**, not mutually exclusive buckets:

- **Selected-tier / earlier review:** earliest numbered pool containing the family. An identity can originate in any tier; separate it before applying ordinary-tier quotas.
- **Canonical family / displayed equation:** 4+7 and 7+4 share an addition family; 4×7 and 7×4 share a multiplication family. 8−3 and 3−8 do not, nor 12÷3 and 12÷4. For bonds, 3+?=10 and ?+3=10 share the same known/missing-part family; 7+?=10 is a different missing-part question.
- **Exact repeat:** the same displayed question occurs again. **Family repeat:** an equivalent canonical family recurs, including reversed display variants. Repeats within one flight and across a Practice Again boundary are reported separately.
- **Same-answer similarity:** different families have the same answer, e.g. 8−3 then 9−4, or 26÷2 then 39÷3. Neither pair is an exact repeat or identity pair.
- **Same identity-pattern similarity:** different questions share an identity flag, e.g. 4−4 then 5−5; or 7+0 then 8+0. This is how a run can feel repetitive despite exact-repeat protection.
- **Shared operand:** consecutive families share an operand. This is expected in chosen-table practice and not automatically undesirable. The fixed total is excluded from the bonds comparison so every question does not falsely count as structurally similar.
- **Neighboring pattern:** different canonical families have corresponding operands no more than one apart, e.g. 4−4 then 5−5; unary activities compare their base, and bonds compare the known part. This is an exploratory structural marker, not a reason to ban useful neighboring examples. Multiplication uses sorted canonical operands, not displayed orientation.
- **Carrying/borrowing:** column regrouping in addition/subtraction. **Round operand:** an operand divisible by ten. **Small answer:** result below ten. These are overlapping structural descriptors, not identities and not proof of low effort.
- **Familiar factor:** a multiplication family involving 2, 5 or 10; tracked without calling it a freebie. Likewise, close subtraction operands can give a small answer while still requiring useful practice.

Quick, Free, Timed and Custom Test are session formats over these activity facts, not new fact types. School Test uses its original multiplication preset; chosen tables retain explicitly requested families; missed-fact review reuses the original activity/scoped fact with fresh answer state. No negative results, fractional division, division by zero, or future non-math activities are implied by this glossary.

## 2. Current selection and sequence behavior

- Validity and balanced family rounds are already present. The generator normally avoids the last three families and balances displayed variants. Tiny pools relax spacing with bounded fallback.
- There is no selected-tier quota, separate L1/L2 review allocation, identity ceiling, same-answer penalty or identity-run limit today. Distinct identity families can appear consecutively.
- Higher levels are not uniformly cumulative. Most L4/L5 pools are separate ranges; injecting a blanket earlier-review allocation would broaden and dilute them.
- Original School Test and chosen-table practice are separate from this numbered-level audit. Do not apply proposed ordinary-practice quotas to them.

## 5. Level 4/5 structural audit

Exact tiers identify range overlap, not every potentially simple structure. The following structural flags overlap and must not be summed; they are not diagnoses of cognitive difficulty.

- Addition L4 has exactly one earlier family: 10 + 10 (1/4,095 = 0.0244%). No identities. It has 1,000 no-regrouping families (24.42%) and 3,095 with regrouping (75.58%). This does not resemble the L3 dilution problem.
- Subtraction L4 has 11 earlier families, all 20 minus 10–20 (11/4,040 = 0.2723%). There are 80 equal-operand identities (1.98%), including 20−20 in that earlier subset. There are 2,420 no-borrow families (59.90%), 1,620 with borrowing (40.10%), and 800 single-digit answers (19.80%). A small answer or no borrowing does not automatically make a fact useless; this is the main L4 structural balance to review separately.
- Addition and subtraction L5 contain no earlier-tier families or identities. Each has 27.50% without regrouping/borrowing and 72.50% with it. Subtraction has only 45 single-digit answers out of 81,000 (0.0556%).
- Multiplication L4/L5 are wholly selected-tier, with no identities. Factors 2, 5 or 10 occur in 24/88 and 237/869 families (27.27% each), respectively. Do not relabel these as universal freebies.
- Division, bonds, doubles, halves and squares L4/L5 are wholly selected-tier with no identities. Squares L4 has only eight families: repetition is inherent in that scope, not evidence of earlier-level leakage.

**Recommendation:** preserve L4/L5 pools and their current broad balance first. Track no-borrow, same-answer, round-operand and small-answer runs as structural diagnostics. Do not introduce new structural quotas or exclude valid families solely because the answer is small. If the owner wants a more demanding L4 subtraction balance, review a separate within-pool borrowing proposal; it is not required to fix the demonstrated L2/L3 dilution.

The repeat-chain sample exposes an exact-boundary issue in Squares L1: **109 of 384 boundaries** repeat the previous flight's final square. This is a proposed fix, not a change made during the audit. It was not found in the private supplied history; do not confuse this synthetic counterexample with an observed learner event.

Runs of distinct identities still occur: sampled maximum eight in Addition L1, seven in Subtraction L1, five in Addition/Subtraction L2, and four in Addition/Subtraction/Multiplication L3. This demonstrates why exact-repeat checks and tier balance alone do not cover all repetition concerns.

Proposed sequence policy alongside the allocation proposal:

- Preserve family balance and exact-repeat avoidance; extend immediate-repeat boundary checks to the preceding final equation/family where alternatives exist. Test whole chains, not only individual flights.
- Normally avoid more than two consecutive identity facts, including in introductory pools where this can be done without removing essential identities. Table 1 and genuinely incompatible tiny pools need explicit exceptions.
- Apply a soft penalty before a third consecutive same answer or same identity pattern, rather than banning all same-answer pairs. Keep arithmetic validity and intended scope/allocation ahead of soft structure rules.
- Treat shared operands and neighboring patterns as diagnostics, not hard prohibitions; selected tables and useful contrast practice intentionally share structure.
- Report distinct coverage and repeat counts separately. Squares L4 necessarily repeats at least 12 families-as-encounters in a 20-question flight because its pool has only eight families; the issue is spacing, not eliminating repetitions.
- Audit both actual current behavior and proposed allocator behavior with these same metrics before claiming improvement. A stronger selected-tier quota in tiny pools will increase repeats; that is a disclosed tradeoff.

## 7. Proposed balance for review — not yet approved or implemented

This proposal is a refinement to review against the earlier accepted 65/25/10 starting policy. It does **not** silently replace that policy. Percentages below are mutually exclusive: identity facts are removed from all tier buckets first. “Identity 5%” means a ceiling, never a requirement to add identities. Unused identity capacity goes to the selected-tier non-identity bucket. Never broaden a pool to fill a review bucket.

| Activity/scope | Selected-tier ordinary facts | Previous-level ordinary review | Older ordinary review | Identity ceiling | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| All L1 | Retain balanced eligible families | None | None | No new cap initially | Preserve introductory zero/identity practice and complete coverage |
| Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, doubles, halves, squares L2 | 75% L2 | 20% L1 | None | 5% | Make the selected level dominant while retaining deliberate review |
| Same activities L3 except squares | 75% L3 | 15% L2 | 5% L1 | 5% | Explicitly weight recent review more than oldest content |
| Squares L3 | 60% L3 | 25% L2 | 10% L1 | 5% | More practice on 11²/12² without making them three quarters of every cycle |
| Bonds L2–3 | At least 95% selected-level non-identity | None | None | 5% | Totals are distinct; do not inject earlier totals merely to meet a ratio |
| All L4–5 | Preserve current valid pool balance | No added earlier content | No added earlier content | No quota; keep existing rare identities unboosted | Already focused; retain tiny existing overlaps, audit structures separately |

The L2 recommendation changes the earlier “retain balanced rounds” proposal, and the L3 recommendations change the earlier 65/25/10 default. Both need explicit acceptance before implementation. L1 preservation is deliberate, not an omission.

Nominal 20-question cycles make the tradeoffs concrete:

- Ordinary L2: 15 selected-tier, 4 L1 review, at most 1 identity.
- Ordinary L3: 15 selected-tier, 3 L2 review, 1 L1 review, at most 1 identity.
- Squares L3: 12 selected-tier, 5 L2 review, 2 L1 review, at most 1 identity. With only two selected-tier families, that means six encounters per new base across a full balanced cycle. A 65% target would mean 13 across those same two bases; 75% would mean 15. None can also promise novelty or a three-family gap throughout.
- Bonds L2–3: 19 selected-tier non-identity, at most 1 identity; no earlier-total injection.

For five-question flights, evaluate progress over a cycle spanning several flights; do not claim each flight realizes exact percentages. Interleave selected material throughout the cycle so an ordinary five-question flight normally contains 3–4 selected-tier questions and squares L3 normally contains 3, when all slots are encountered in order. This is an allocator acceptance target, not a current result. Interrupted worksheets, skips, revisits, Undo and reload need explicit encounter/reservation semantics before coding; do not promise exact encountered quotas when a learner stops selectively.

Within buckets, retain balanced family rounds and variant rotation. Avoid adjacent identical equations where alternatives exist, ordinarily avoid more than two consecutive identities, and prefer variety of answers/structures. Tiny focus pools must relax recent-family spacing before violating validity or silently changing allocation. Do not exclude every nearby operand or treat all familiar fact families as waste.
